# GPU test plan stages 4–6 on the MSOE GB10: custom fingers, contract v2, the GPU path

Real twin (Isaac Sim), **not** the mock. The first run of stages 4–6 on any machine (Brev stopped at stage 3). Same
machine, install and code as the [GB10 bring-up](2026-10-20-gb10-sim-server-bringup.md).

## Question

Do custom fingers swap in and behave plausibly (stage 4)? Does contract v2's physics (in-hand readout, mesh parts, part
friction, the grip-force limit, transport) agree with arithmetic (stage 5)? Does the vectorised GPU path give the
reference path's answers, and how fast is it (stage 6)? The optimizer's scores are only as good as these.

## Setup

- **Compute:** MSOE GB10 (`spark1.ai.msoe.edu`; DGX Spark, GB10, driver 580.178.04), free. No other team's processes on
  the GPU. About 11:38–12:10 CDT.
- **Isaac Sim 6.0.1** (aarch64 pip install, `~/isaac-sim-6.0.1`); sim server on uvicorn 0.29.0, port 9011. Code
  `fd6fbdd`.
- **Servers** (all `--friction-combine average` unless noted; the scene settings as in the bring-up log):
  - reference, DEBUG: `--envs 1 --log-level DEBUG` (stages 4 and 5);
  - `--friction-combine max` (5.3's binding check);
  - `--envs 64`, with GPU physics by default;
  - `--envs 1 --device cuda:0`;
  - `--envs 64 --device cpu`;
  - `--envs 256`.
- **Batches:** the runbook's files. `flat_pads.json` (stock, `pad_x0`, `pad_x2` on the 10 mm cube),
  `stock_cube_transport.json`, `mesh_l_bracket.json` (2 convex boxes, 4.3 g, aluminium-on-PLA friction),
  `heavy_block_grip_force.json` (300 g, 10 × 40 × 40 mm, μ 0.08, flat pads, 15 N), `stock_cube.json`. Plus friction
  variants of the heavy block, and diagnostic friction and mass sweeps (in Results).

- **The GPU path with copies is deterministic.** A repeat of the 64-copy grids agreed 81/81 and 162/162. The 256-copy
  run agrees with the 64-copy one at 78/81 (cube) and 239/243 (flat pads).
- **For the cube, only GPU physics *with* copies departs from the reference.** GPU physics alone, and copies on CPU
  physics, each agree with the reference about as well as the reference agrees with itself (93.8–95.1%). Together,
  they hold about 9 more of 81 grasps, every time.
- **The bracket disagrees on every alternative path** (70–84%). The reference bracket grid was run only once, so its
  own repeatability, and how much of this is the bracket tumbling, is unknown.
- **The rest of contract v2 agrees on the 64-copy GPU path:**
  - in hand: 18/18 (median differences 0.35 mm dx, 0.12 mm dy, 0.42° yaw; max 0.52 mm and 4.8°);
  - cube transport: 18/18;
  - heavy block at 15 N, `none` and with transport: 3/3 each (all dropped).
  - The exception is the μ 0.9 block at 15 N: 1/3 on GPU against 3/3 on the reference.
- **Flat-pad grid on the 64-copy GPU path:** 216/243 (88.9%). The GPU holds more for every design: stock 67 vs 58,
  `pad_x0` 61 vs 55, `pad_x2` 66 vs 56. It mixes designs within a chunk without errors.

## Failures observed

1. **Landing capture makes tight and loose pads score alike** (stage 4). `pad_x0` should leave about 1 mm of slack per
   side, yet it held the cube at dx ±3.5 mm and even at dx ±8 mm on the grid. There the cube spans 3–13 mm and one pad
   face (at 6 mm) has to land on top of it, and the cube still ends centred in the hand. The position-controlled descent
   pushes a part a finger lands on into the gap, as the stock fingers did 8–12 mm out on Brev (that log's Failure 4).
   Not confirmed directly: the server records the part's pose only at spawn. Until a part disturbed during the descent
   is detected, scores flatter every design, and the comparison between designs is blunted.
2. **The twin squeezes with only about 2.5 N per jaw** (stage 5). Every heavy-block expectation in 5.3–5.5 assumed about
   40 N: `jaw_close_mm` 2.0 commands the pads 1 mm past the block's faces, into a 4e4 N/m drive. The friction and mass
   sweeps say about 2.5 N. That 2.5 N holds with or without a force limit, and at 30 N too. So it isn't the
   closing-speed caveat in 5.4; it's the squeeze itself: the close target, the drive's stiffness, or contact offsets.
   Parts up to about 100 g are fine at μ 0.3. Anything heavier, or slippery, will score as dropped regardless of design.
   Not investigated in code.
3. **The GPU path with copies is biased optimistic for the cube** (stage 6). It holds about 9/81 more grasps than the
   reference, repeatably. GPU physics alone and CPU copies alone don't do this. The runbook names CCD being off on the
   GPU path as a suspect. Until it's understood, the CPU path with copies (`--envs 64 --device cpu`) is the fast path that
   matches the reference, at about 5 grasps/s.
4. **Isaac Sim sometimes fails to start on the GB10:** 3 of 12 starts today.
   - Two hung before Kit logged anything: 2 threads asleep on a lock, 0% CPU, about 70 MB resident. One was in tmux,
     one detached.
   - One segfaulted 1.2 s in, in NVIDIA's `libnvidia-eglcore.so.580.178.04`, called from Kit's Vulkan graphics plugin.
     Kit saved a minidump under `~/isaac-sim-6.0.1/venv/.../isaacsim/kit/data/Kit/Isaac-Sim Python/6.0/` and tried to
     upload it (Kit's default).
   - Every retry worked. The bring-up log's tmux theory is wrong: it's GPU/Vulkan start-up. A watchdog handles it: no
     `Simulation App Startup Complete` within 90 s means kill and retry.
   - The Carbonite named semaphore in `/dev/shm` was checked and is not the cause: free, with no waiters.
5. An SSH drop killed one remote script mid-run; the sim server survived (`setsid`). Long runs now go through
   `setsid nohup` scripts on the GB10.
